ONBOARDING · KEY TERMS

Key terms

Everything on this page is built from these five ideas — this is what each one means and why it exists.

💊

DOS — Days of Supply

How many days a single fill is assumed to cover. A 28-day fill "runs out" 28 days after it's picked up.

e.g. A Venclexta fill on Jan 5 with a 28-day supply covers the patient through Feb 2. Note: a missing or zero days-supply falls back to 28 days. Medical-benefit (PX) claims use a per-product value instead, and orals correctly get NULL there, not a default.
⏳

Grace period

Extra days beyond DOS you're still willing to call "still on treatment," before deciding someone stopped. Longer grace tolerates later refills.

e.g. Their next fill doesn't happen until Feb 20 — 18 days late. Well inside a 60-day grace, so it's still the same episode. Note: a product nobody has reviewed yet gets NULL for both DOS and grace — never a default — so it can't silently create or extend a line of therapy.
🔗

Episode

A run of fills for the same drug, glued together as long as the gap between one Rx's start date and the next stays inside DOS combined with Grace. A bigger gap starts a new episode.

e.g. Steady refills every 28 days all join into one episode. A 90+ day gap between Rx start dates blows past DOS combined with Grace (88 days) — the next fill starts a new episode instead.
🧩

Regimen

Whichever drug(s) are active at the same time. Changes the moment a drug starts or stops overlapping another. With more than one drug active, the highest-priority one — the backbone — decides how the regimen is classified.

e.g. Venclexta alone is one regimen. The moment Azacitidine is added alongside it, the regimen becomes "Venclexta + Azacitidine" — and since Venclexta outranks Azacitidine on the priority list, Venclexta is the backbone.
🧵

Line of therapy (LOT)

A run of regimens that counts as one continuous course of treatment. Starts a new line on a 90+ day gap, or a backbone change — unless it's specifically exempted, like switching between Venclexta and an HMA.

e.g. That same patient stops treatment, then restarts Venclexta 120 days later. Past the 90-day threshold — that restart is Line 2, not a continuation of Line 1.

KPIs

Reported metrics built from the terms above.

📊

NPS — New Patient Starts

How many patients begin their very first line, by regimen, by month. Counted once per patient, ever — at their first line's start, not at every later switch.

e.g. If that patient's very first-ever fill was in January, they count toward January's Venclexta NPS — once, for good, no matter how many lines follow.

REFERENCE · BACKBONE PRIORITY

Which drug wins? The backbone hierarchy

When a regimen has more than one drug active, the backbone is whichever one ranks highest on a fixed priority list — and AML uses two different lists, depending on the patient.

Which list applies?If a patient has ever received an Intensive Chemo product, they're IC-eligible for their whole journey and use the 26-product list. Otherwise they're IC-ineligible and use the shorter 13-product list — this is decided once per patient, not per regimen.
